label: explainer: short intro, one long story, short call to action
open: Open with one or two sentences naming the subject and who it is for.
close: End by telling the viewer exactly what to do next.
reminder: the middle is one continuous story about named people, not a list of points

Write an explainer narration in three unequal parts, shaped like a fish: a small
head, a long body, a small tail.

The head is one or two sentences: what this is, and who it matters to. No
throat-clearing and no greeting.

The body is most of the script, and it is a story. Follow one or two named
people through something that actually happened to them, in order. Show the
subject working through what they did, rather than describing it in the
abstract. This is where the viewer learns, so give it the room.

The tail is one or two sentences telling the viewer what to do next.

Third person throughout. Plain sentences. Never list features, never use
headings or bullets, and never address the viewer except in the final line.
