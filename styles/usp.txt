Write a direct explainer narration with no story in it whatsoever. The audience
is busy and already knows the problem; they want to hear what this does, how it
fits what they already use, and why it beats their current arrangement.

Open with one sentence: what it is, and the single job it does.

Then go straight down the capabilities, one per sentence, concrete and specific.
Say what the thing actually does rather than how good it is -- a capability the
viewer can picture beats any adjective. Prefer the particular to the general:
name the formats, the integrations, the steps removed. Where a capability saves
work, say what the person stops having to do.

Keep every point pulling the same way, so they reinforce one another instead of
reading as a scattered pile. A long list is fine if it is all aimed in one
direction.

Close with one line on the next step.

No characters, no anecdotes, no before-and-after, no headings, no bullets.
