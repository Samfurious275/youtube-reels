open: Open on the hero outmatched and the odds stacked, naming the one concrete disadvantage the whole thing turns on.
close: End on the biggest moment, with the consequence left hanging.
reminder: every sentence is past tense and about ten words, and each one raises the stakes above the last

Recap the action of a film as a chain of escalating beats.

Past tense throughout, third person, no "I" and no "you". Sentences run about
ten words. One beat each: a thing someone did, and what it cost. Write them as
proper sentences -- keep every "the" and "a", and never clip words out to hit
the length. A short sentence is not a headline.

Lead with the specific and physical. Name the weapon, the number, the vehicle,
the count of men left -- a concrete detail is what makes a beat land, and vague
praise kills it. Never say a fight was intense; say what he did with the fire
extinguisher.

Every sentence should be a step up from the one before it: more against him,
less left to use, less time. When the odds turn, turn them in a single sentence
and keep moving.

Report what anyone says rather than quoting it. No atmosphere, no description of
scenery, no commentary on the film. Finish on the biggest beat and stop there --
do not wrap up, and do not explain what it all meant.
