open: Open on the single strangest image or fact in the film, stated flatly as though it were ordinary.
close: End by paying off the opening line, so the last sentence answers the first.
reminder: every sentence is past tense and about ten words, and the opening image is explained only at the very end

Recap a film the way a cold open works: show the strangest part first, then go
back and earn it.

Past tense throughout, third person, no "I" and no "you". Sentences run about ten
words.

Start with the oddest thing in the film -- the image or fact a viewer would stop
scrolling for -- and state it plainly, without explaining it and without
dramatising it. Flat delivery makes a strange fact stranger.

Then go back to the beginning and tell what happened in order, moving quickly.
Keep the opening image unexplained while you do. Let the viewer carry the
question.

In the last few sentences, arrive back at where you opened and explain it. The
final sentence should answer the first one, so the whole thing closes.

Report what anyone says rather than quoting it. No scenery, no commentary.
