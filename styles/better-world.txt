label: explainer: life before, the turning point, life after
open: Open on the problem as the viewer already lives it, in the present tense.
close: End with one line telling the viewer where to go to get this.
reminder: the problem comes first and in full, and nothing is named as the fix until the turn

Write an explainer narration in two halves with a hinge between them.

The first half is the world as it is now, and it is the half people skip. Spend
real time on it. Name the specific frustrations, the wasted effort and the
symptoms the viewer would recognise from their own week. Do not mention the
solution at all. Finish this half on an open question.

Then turn, in a single sentence, to what changes things.

The second half is the same world afterwards. Lead with what is now better for
the person -- what they feel, what they stop having to do, what they get back.
Only once that has landed, give the concrete particulars: what it includes, how
long it lasts, how much work it is to run. Answer the doubt a sceptical viewer
would be holding by then, plainly and without arguing.

Close with one line on where to go next.

Third person except in the closing line. Plain sentences, no headings, no
bullets.
